# ICAI Practical Guide — Appeal Drafting Principles

Source consulted: ICAI, *Practical Guide to GST Adjudication and Appeals including GSTAT*, First Edition, April 2026, updated to 31 March 2026. Public PDF supplied by user: https://d23z1tp9il9etb.cloudfront.net/download/pdf26/Practical%20Guide%20to%20GST%20Adjudication%20and%20Appeals%20including%20GSTAT.pdf

This file is a concise operational summary for agent use. Do not reproduce the source guide wholesale; cite the guide and verify current law, forum rules, and portal instructions before filing.

## Appeal-stage intake checklist

Before drafting, confirm:

- Exact date of communication/receipt of the impugned order, with proof, because limitation runs from receipt/communication.
- Whether the matter should be accepted, appealed, or partly appealed.
- Forum and form: first appeal generally in APL-01; GSTAT appeal generally in APL-05, subject to current portal/rules.
- Demand breakup: admitted/undisputed dues, disputed tax, interest, penalty, fine, fee, refund rejected, demand dropped.
- Limitation: first appeal timeline should be treated as calendar months, not a rough day count; condonation is limited and must be separately pleaded where needed.
- Pre-deposit: undisputed dues must be discharged and statutory disputed-tax/penalty pre-deposit must be made; shortfall can make the appeal defective/void.
- Certified copy / uploaded copy requirements, authorization, fees, document sequence, pagination, and verification.

## Statement of facts

The statement of facts should narrate only relevant facts leading to the demand/order. Use this structure:

1. Appellant profile and registration.
2. Period and transactions involved.
3. Audit/investigation/SCN chronology.
4. SCN allegations and proposed demand.
5. Reply, evidence, and hearing history.
6. Findings in the impugned order.
7. Demand confirmed/dropped or refund rejected.
8. Filing facts: limitation, pre-deposit, authorization, and annexures.

Keep facts separate from grounds and arguments.

## Grounds of appeal

Draft grounds to expose adjudication failure, not to argue the whole case. Classify every ground into one or more of these failure modes:

- Misunderstanding or non-consideration of facts/evidence.
- Misapplication or misinterpretation of law.
- Failure to follow binding judicial authority, circulars, or Board instructions.
- Order travelling beyond the SCN.
- Violation of natural justice or non-speaking order.
- Computation or classification error.

Do not make grounds over-dependent on one current case citation. Case law can change before hearing; phrase the ground around the legal proposition and authority hierarchy, then use detailed submissions to discuss decisions.

## Grounds versus arguments

- **Ground:** concise reason the order is wrong and why appellate intervention is needed.
- **Argument/submission:** detailed facts, statutory language, evidence, and case-law application supporting that ground.

Bad pattern: long narrative ground with every case and fact embedded.

Good pattern:

```text
Because the impugned order is contrary to the record and law in confirming [demand/penalty] despite [specific evidence/legal condition], which was placed before the adjudicating authority but not considered, and therefore deserves to be set aside.
```

Then explain the full reasoning under written submissions.

## Link appeal to SCN and reply

For each ground, map:

```text
SCN allegation -> Reply/evidence filed -> Finding in order -> Error in finding -> Relief requested
```

If the order introduces a new basis not proposed in SCN, raise a separate ground that the order travels beyond the notice.

## Additional evidence and additional grounds

When new evidence is needed, explain why it was not before the earlier authority and why it is necessary for deciding an existing ground. Typical justifications:

- Evidence was submitted but not admitted/considered.
- Sufficient cause prevented earlier production.
- Earlier opportunity to produce it was not effectively available.
- Evidence directly supports an existing ground.

For additional grounds, prefer including all possible grounds in the original appeal. If a ground was omitted, later leave should be framed as bona fide and necessary to advance the appellant's cause, while recognizing that the respondent may need opportunity to rebut.

## Prayer

Make the prayer specific. Match it to the disputed issues and include alternative relief where appropriate:

- Set aside the impugned order/demand to the extent challenged.
- Delete or recompute tax, interest, penalty, fine, or fee.
- Remand with directions where record consideration or hearing failure is central.
- Grant consequential relief, re-credit/refund, and stay/recovery relief where permissible.
- Grant personal hearing and permission to file additional submissions/evidence if required.

## Presentation and filing discipline

- Follow prescribed forms/formats; use annexures for charts and reconciliations.
- Do not leave form fields unanswered.
- Complete verification and signatures properly.
- Index and paginate the appeal memorandum and annexures.
- Use full designations of authorities; avoid informal abbreviations.
- Ensure every enclosure is complete, legible, and correctly labelled.
- Verify every authority is current and not overruled before citing.
- For GSTAT, check current Procedure Rules/President instructions for document upload sequence, English translation requirements, signatures, certified copies, pre-deposit, and court fees.
